[size=150][b]ForgePlayer (alpha) — one player, every screen and your e-stim, all in sync[/b][/size]

[img]https://forgeplayer.app/forgeplayer_hero.png[/img]

Ever tried to watch a scene across two screens while an e-stim track plays — and spent the whole time fighting windows that drift out of sync? [b]ForgePlayer[/b] is the fix. It's a free desktop player that drives [b]your monitors and your e-stim hardware from one timeline[/b]. One seek bar. Everything stays locked together — through play, pause, scrubbing, and chapter jumps.

You point it at a scene, hit play, and:

[list]
[*][b]Video plays across up to three monitors[/b] at once, all in sync — main screen, a companion angle, a second wall, however you've set it up. Each screen can letterbox or crop-to-fill (handy for ultrawides) and go fullscreen on its own monitor.
[*][b]E-stim plays right alongside the video[/b], driven either from a [b]funscript[/b] (synthesised live) or a [b]pre-rendered audio file[/b]. It supports [b]three-phase stereostim[/b] output for modern e-stim hardware, plus a classic continuous waveform for 312 / 2B-style boxes.
[*][b]Each stim output needs its own USB audio dongle[/b] — separate from your speakers. One drives your main stim; an optional second drives a second device (e.g. a prostate channel). Tested with a [url=https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08LGPKFN5]VENTION USB sound card[/url] and others — any standard USB audio output works.
[*][b]Jump around by chapter.[/b] If a scene ships chapters they show as markers on the seek bar, and you can step through them from the console [i]or[/i] from the bar on the video window itself — no hunting for controls mid-scene.
[*][b]Loop a scene[/b] when you want it to keep going instead of stopping on the last frame. Every screen wraps together, and the jump back to the start is faded so the stim doesn't click on the restart.
[*]It opens [b].forge scene bundles[/b] with a double-click — the packs exported by [url=https://funscriptforge.com]FunscriptForge[/url], our companion authoring app — and also reads loose funscript / audio folders.
[/list]

Point the Library at a folder of scenes and it works out which video goes with which funscripts and stim audio, gives you a thumbnail grid to pick from, and remembers your choices per scene. When a scene has several versions — a 4K and a 1080p, alternate funscript sets — it asks once and then replays your pick.

It's built for the cockpit-style way people actually watch: big screen in front of you, controls on a laptop or a little touchscreen off to the side, hands free. Fullscreen players cover the console, so there's a [b]Console[/b] button on every video window (and Escape) to bring it back without stopping playback.

[size=125][b]What you need (e-stim hardware)[/b][/size]

You'll want [b]one or two USB audio dongles + one or two stim power boxes[/b]. Haptic 1 carries the three-phase / stereostim signal; Haptic 2 an optional prostate signal.

[b]Three-phase / stereostim boxes[/b] (use Pulse-based mode): [url=https://www.stimkits.com/]The Tingler StimKit I[/url] · [url=https://shop.impudicus.net/products/estim-hero-stereo-basic]EstimHero[/url] · [url=https://darkmatter69.com/collections/estim]ZC95 MKII[/url]

[b]Classic boxes[/b] (use Continuous mode): [url=https://erostek.com/products/mk-312bt-power-unit]MK-312BT[/url] · [url=https://estim.store/collections/2b]2B[/url]

[b]Not yet supported:[/b] Coyote (DG-Lab) — it's Bluetooth, which isn't tested yet. [b]Mechanical toys[/b] (strokers, Handy, Keon, OSR) aren't driven either — ForgePlayer is an e-stim player.

[size=125][b]This is an alpha[/b][/size]

ForgePlayer is [b]pre-1.0 software, actively developed[/b] — the current build is [b]v0.1.17-alpha[/b]. It works and it's fun, but expect rough edges — please report anything weird. A few honest caveats:

[list]
[*][b]Windows is the tested platform.[/b] macOS and Linux builds come out of the same CI pipeline but are [b]far less proven[/b].
[*][b]Bluetooth devices haven't been tested yet.[/b] Today's path is audio-based e-stim (a stim box fed from an audio output).
[*][b]HDR passthrough is off.[/b] HDR10 files play, but the HDR renderer crashed on teardown, so it uses the stable one for now — on an HDR-ON display that can look over-bright. Turn Windows HDR off for the playback monitor.
[*][b]It isn't code-signed yet[/b], so Windows SmartScreen will call it an unknown publisher the first time.
[*]e-stim safety: [b]keep electrode placement below the waist, start low, and ramp up.[/b] Use the in-app Calibrate button to set a comfortable level on the box's own knob before you press play — and note that a looped scene doesn't end on its own.
[/list]

Set your audio devices up first — the app opens on the Library, but nothing can route until Setup knows which output is your speakers and which is your stim dongle. There's a [b]Test[/b] button on each one so you can prove the dongle is live before you commit to a scene.
